Welcome, BruControl brewer

This Importer turns your BruControl setup into a Skynet Schema, so your brewery comes up in Skynet with your boards, your Devices, your screens and your scripts. It only reads your BruControl files. BruControl and its files are never changed, so you can keep brewing with BruControl until you are ready.

In Skynet, everything you see and touch (the user interface) is in a browser: Microsoft Edge, Chrome, Firefox, DuckDuckGo, Safari and others.

The Skynet Panel is the brewery program. Its Brain can run on this Windows computer (or any computer with Windows 10 or newer), just like BruControl, or on a Mac or Linux computer, or on a Raspberry Pi. The choice of hardware is up to you, but to start, it can run right here, and you can move it later if you want.

We need your input, and some understanding of how things work in Skynet compared with BruControl. When something needs to be upgraded or changed, for example an Element that becomes a different type of Device in Skynet, we will explain why.

That is the first difference: where you added an Element in BruControl, you add a Device in Skynet. Let's look at the differences now.

BruControl and Skynet

What stays the same

  • Your boards, wiring and cables. The same Arduino Mega, Grand Central, ESP32 and friends, the same USB COM ports, Ethernet and WiFi links, and the same pin numbers. Nothing is rewired.
  • Your Elements, Workspaces and Scripts all come over. Each one is explained below.
  • Your pictures and sounds from the BruControl Media folder.

What is different in Skynet

  • It runs in a browser. The Brain (this PC, a mini PC or a Raspberry Pi) runs Skynet, and you reach it from any PC, Mac, tablet or phone.
  • Some new words:

  • Skynet pictures and Templates. Pumps, valves, pipes and vessels use Skynet's own drawings, with pipes that show the flow.
  • Everything comes in the Skynet way. Names, settings and script lines are changed to Skynet's words and style (for example a 0-255 output becomes 0-100%). Each change is listed for you.
  • Firmware. Each board gets Skynet firmware once, over its USB cable, from inside Skynet. No Arduino IDE.

Your Elements

Pumps, Valves, Heating Elements and such come into Skynet as Devices, using the same hardwiring, the same Interfaces and the same assigned Pin / Port.

BruControl made a major change with Firmware 46, where the Mega ports A0 to A15 were changed to ports 54 to 69. If you have Firmware 46, it imports fine. If you did NOT upgrade to BruControl Firmware 46, the older A0 to A15 import fine too. In Skynet you can refer to those ports either way, as they are the same.

Additionally, Skynet has subclasses of Devices (Elements in BruControl). In BruControl an On/Off Pump and a Motorized Ball Valve were both DO type ports. They are also the general class DO in Skynet, but with the subclasses DO:Pump and DO:Motorized Valve. While in Skynet you could build a Pump or Valve in almost the same way as in BruControl, in Skynet you can push a button to create a Pump or Valve, and it has many of the features you had in your BruControl Pump or Valve, so the Skynet Pump or Valve is the better choice. Generally a Pump or Valve will be updated to a new Skynet one, because of its built-in features (and less coding in a Process, your Script).

Your Workspaces

Your Workspaces are created as browser Tabs. A Workspace and a Tab are very similar and generally have the same attributes. They are like a container that holds Devices (your BruControl Elements) and Widgets (your Timers, Switches and similar application Elements). Globals are replaced by a Text Widget, a Value Widget and so on. Globals that were strictly variables become a gGlobal, which is a variable with global scope (across Processes).

Your Scripts

Your Scripts are translated to Processes in Skynet. The architecture is similar, with some differences, as Skynet coding follows some more standard coding conventions. While similar, it is much easier in Skynet to write a Process. There are some new commands that you can use. There are also other standard coding methods, like for and while, that can be used. If you don't know those, don't worry, as you may never use them, and they will not be in your new code.

Names

Both BruControl and Skynet use a Name to control a Device (Element). In BruControl, once created, you could not change the Name, but it is easy to do in Skynet, as it updates it everywhere (in Processes too!).

Skynet has a preferred naming convention, which the Oakbarn Brewery used. The naming convention lets you (the user) know the Interface and port, plus the Type (Digital Output, Temperature Probe type, Hysteresis, PID and so on), then what it is: the Label (displayname). The last part of a Name tells you the Tab (Workspace). While at first it may seem somewhat convoluted, you may find it familiar and useful, but that is strictly up to you. You can give your own Name rather than the suggested one when you add a new Device (Element).

When importing, your BruControl Names are changed to the Skynet naming convention, like everything else that comes in (Skynet Rule 15.1). Every change is listed in the import report, and Processes are updated to the new Names. You may choose to keep your BruControl Names instead, but we will ask you twice, because the Skynet way is better in the end: one look at a Name tells you the Interface, port, type, Label and Tab, and every new Device you add will have a Skynet Name anyway.

Example: MB_A0_10KNTC_Mash~Tun_SP  =  Interface MB, port A0, type 10KNTC (a 10K thermistor probe), Label Mash~Tun, Tab SP.

DisplayName is the Label

The displayname is called the Label in Skynet. Label better says what it is. Unlike BruControl, the Label is an independent field and must be filled in: in BruControl it was optional, in Skynet it is mandatory. Don't worry when importing: if the BruControl displayname was blank, BruControl just showed the Element Name, so the Skynet import puts the BruControl Name into the Label and does not show the Label on the Tab (its .label_visible is false; the item itself is still shown).

Skynet keeps hidden and locked as separate settings (BruControl's hiddenlocked is not of much use in Skynet). In Skynet .visible is true or false. When your Scripts or Elements are imported, visibility is updated to the new format automatically.

Global Elements

This is probably the most difficult and time-consuming part of importing into Skynet. Global Elements in BruControl served four separate purposes, although a single Global could do all four:

  1. Database: it was sent to the database every clock cycle. It made millions of database entries very fast. The Oakbarn Brewery used a lot of Globals, and with the local database installed it would reach gigabytes in days, so the Oakbarn Brewery never used the database.
  2. API: the only way to talk to the outside world. The Oakbarn Brewery used it to work with Node-RED.
  3. An Element on a Workspace, to display a value, text or a JPG file.
  4. A Global variable with global scope: not limited to a single Script, it could be used in any Script.

Skynet handles the four purposes as separate items:

1. Database

Any Device (Element) or Widget (Timers and similar Elements) can be sent to the database. Each item has its own key and its own frequency. You can still send every millisecond, but there are lots of choices (every second, so many seconds, every minute and so on). You control how much data is sent, and even when it is sent. An item can also belong to one or more Groups: it goes to the database while one of its Groups is started, and stops when the Group is stopped. A Mash Group is a good example.

In BruControl, to send a Temperature Probe to the database you had to:
  1. Create a Global Element.
  2. Write a Script to keep that Global set to the Mash Temperature Probe value.
  3. It went to the database all the time, making millions of database points.
In Skynet it is quite different:
  1. Set the Mash Probe's database setting from Off to something like Every minute.
  2. Create the Group Mash (only once) and put the Mash Probe in it. You can put other things in it too: an electronic pH meter in the Mash could be recorded during the Mash as well.
  3. Start the Group with start_db_group Mash in a Process.
  4. The Probe (and pH meter) are recorded at the frequency you set (every minute). A 60 minute Mash gives only 60 data points.
  5. Stop the Group with stop_db_group Mash in a Process.

2. API

This is now a separate attribute (property). Any item (Device or Widget) can be set up to talk to an outside program like Node-RED. There is no need to create and set a Global in between, as in BruControl: the Device or Widget itself is read.

3. Element displayed on a Workspace

You may have used a Global to show something like the Mash Probe on more than one Workspace; the Oakbarn Brewery did, and it also used Global Elements to display text or a picture (JPG) file. Those uses are available in Skynet, but not as a Global type Element: there are Text Widgets to display text, Value Widgets to display a value, and Picture Widgets to display a picture file. Generally the background scripting works the same in BruControl and Skynet, but in Skynet you can change the background path (where on the computer the picture file is). Skynet accepts other formats than .jpg and tries to make an SVG copy for display. The original file is never changed. An SVG keeps the picture sharp when it is made bigger or smaller. This will need your input when a Global is imported.

4. Global variable

In Skynet it is simply a variable with global scope, called a gGlobal. Its Kind shows in the start of its name (gS_ for a String, gV_ for a Value and others). Don't get bogged down with this; it will become clear later.

A gGlobal behaves just like a variable, with the same attributes, but with two very distinct differences:

  1. It can be used in any Process (Script). This is called global scope.
  2. It is non-volatile: it keeps its value and attributes (properties) even when the computer is turned off.

In BruControl you made a variable named GobblyGook with new value GobblyGook. In Skynet it is the same, and you can also set it on the same line: new value GobblyGook = 10. In BruControl you needed a Global Element to have a global variable; in Skynet a gGlobal is a variable with global scope, and you declare it in a Process with a different declaration:

new gGlobalValue gV_GobblyGook = 10
new gV gV_GobblyGook = 10          (the short form)
new value gV_GobblyGook            (a name starting gV_ makes it a gGlobal)

All three make the same gGlobal. When you type gGlobalValue, the gV_ is filled in for you; you type the rest of the name and, if you want, = 10 to set its first value.

So there is a lot to digest in how Globals go from BruControl to Skynet, but we do them one by one, and you decide how each is handled in Skynet. After a few questions, the Importer tells you what it thinks you should do, and you can accept it or choose a different option.

How we will guide you

  1. Pick your BruControl config file. We look in Documents\BruControl first, or a folder you choose.
  2. Boards. We show each BruControl board and its COM port or address, so you can check them.
  3. Tabs and items, one question at a time. Each workspace becomes a Tab. For each BruControl item we ask you a few plain questions (is this a pump, a valve, a vessel? what kind?) so it becomes the right Skynet Device, Widget or Template.
  4. Processes. Your scripts are changed to Skynet Processes, and any line that needs a look is shown.
  5. Save the Schema and send it to your Skynet Brain, with a report of everything that changed.

Nothing is saved or changed until the last step, and you can go Back at any time.
